The **Terrain** tool sculpts the shared landscape directly in the viewport. The
same height data is used in top-down, 2D Iso and 3D views.

Select the hill icon in the tool strip, then choose **Raise**, **Lower** or
**Smooth**. Drag across the ground or hold the pointer still to keep applying the
brush. Adjust **Radius** and **Strength** with the panel's minus/plus buttons.
**[ / ]** also change radius; **Shift** temporarily switches to Lower. Lower
stops at height zero. Each stroke is one undo step, and **Esc** cancels an active
stroke. Camera pan, orbit and zoom remain available through their normal controls.

The circular cursor follows the terrain rather than walls or props. The brush
can adjust an existing hill made with terrain control vertices; those vertices
remain available for precise editing through **Edit Vertex**.

MZ ground materials and painted path compositions stay in world coordinates as
the landscape changes. Use the Rect tool to paint and compose a winding path
over a hill. Stairs, walls and upper floors retain their own geometry and
construction heights. Select a ground MZ material before sculpting a new map to
use it as the default ground; otherwise a neutral ground appears until a material
is painted.

## Shape Terrain with geometry

Use the **Linedef / Edge** and **Sector / Face** tools in a top-down or **2D Iso**
view to draw editable terrain guides. Select a line or sector, open its
**Edit Linedef** or **Edit Sector** action, and edit the `[terrain]` section in
its right-hand action parameters. Set `enabled = true` (**Shape Terrain**) to
make the geometry influence the landscape. The same terrain
is rendered in 3D and used for ground picking, walking and shadows.

### Graded corridors

For a linedef, set:

- **Start height** and **End height**: absolute ground altitudes at the linedef's
  start and end. Height is interpolated along its length. Give adjoining segments
  matching endpoint heights to form a connected graded route.
- **Corridor width**: the full width of the graded strip. Half lies on either
  side of the line. A zero width makes a ridge-like guide.
- **Edge blend**: the additional distance on each side and around the endpoints
  over which the strip joins the surrounding landscape. Larger values make
  gentler slopes.

The linedef action parameters use:

```toml
[terrain]
enabled = true
start_height = 0.0
end_height = 1.2
width = 1.8
blend = 2.5
```

Leave the action's other sections in place when editing these values.

Draw several connected segments for a winding approach. Each segment retains
its own height settings. Moving its vertices or dragging the whole line reshapes
the ground immediately. Endpoint heights are stored on the line; they do not
move the vertices vertically or turn them into independent hill controls.

### Plateaus and hills

Close a polygon with the Linedef tool, then select the resulting sector:

- **Plateau** fills the polygon at **Target height**, with a smooth transition
  outside its boundary over **Edge blend** world units. It works for a building
  site, terrace or clearing, and follows concave boundaries.
- **Hill** rises inside the boundary. Its edge meets the surrounding ground;
  the interior reaches full influence at **Edge blend** units from the nearest
  boundary. A narrow shape with a large blend distance therefore has a lower
  peak. Reduce the blend distance for a fuller top.

For example, the sector action uses:

```toml
[terrain]
enabled = true
mode = "Plateau" # "Plateau" or "Hill"
height = 1.2
blend = 2.5
```

Target heights are absolute world altitudes. A target below the surrounding
landscape lowers it. Ground is clamped at zero. Overlapping sector targets blend
without depending on storage order; graded corridors then shape their routes
across those regions. Terrain brush strokes remain a final layer of world-space
sculpt offsets, so sculpting can further adjust an enabled shape.

The separate `floor_height` parameter (**Floor / tile height**) places an elevated tile or floor.
It does not enable a terrain modifier. Ordinary named areas continue to act as
logical regions until **Shape Terrain** is enabled. Disabling the modifier removes
its influence and keeps the geometry and settings available for reuse.

MZ terrain materials, tile overrides, painted paths and organic stamps stay in
world coordinates and follow the resulting ground height. Shape Terrain controls
height. Use the selected geometry's `[terrain.material]` TOML section for a
material that follows the guide, with path width or area inset and soft/noisy
edges. Rect and Paint strokes remain at their authored world positions.
See [terrain material settings](procedural_materials#terrain-materials-from-geometry-toml)
for complete path, verge and rocky-area examples.
Each inspector edit and geometry move uses normal map undo/redo. Save/reopen
retains the guide and its parameters; no generated terrain mesh must be saved.

### Try Unending Night

Open `test_projects/UnendingNight.eldiron`. The scene starts in daylight with a
wider view of the inn and its eastern grounds. Follow the worn MZ trail from
about `(12.5, 12.5)` toward the new terrain examples:

1. **Terrain Plateau**, around `(21.5, 14)`: select the named sector in the
   region tree, change **Target height** or **Edge blend**, and walk across it.
2. **Terrain Hill**, around `(23, 23)`: compare its inward slope with the plateau's
   outside transition. Move a corner to change the hill's footprint.
3. **Terrain approach 1–5**: use the Linedef tool to select the graded segments
   beneath the winding trail. Change an endpoint height or width, or move an
   endpoint. Walk up and back down; the MZ path stays on the terrain.

Switch to the **3D** camera to inspect the same surface; the saved camera looks
at the examples. Authoring sectors and linedefs uses the 2D/iso views, while the
Terrain brush also sculpts directly in 3D. Terrain resolution is shared through
`terrain_subdivisions`; enabling Shape Terrain uses at least four subdivisions
per world unit. All cameras consume the same facets, including sculpt strokes
and terrain exclusions.

See [Procedural Materials](procedural_materials) for ground and path authoring,
and [Creating 3D Maps](creating_3d_maps) for buildings and other geometry.
